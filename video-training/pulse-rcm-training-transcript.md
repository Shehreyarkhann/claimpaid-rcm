# Pulse RCM — Full Training Video Transcript

_Runtime: ~6.6 minutes · 16 sections_

## 01-intro

Welcome to Pulse RCM training. Pulse is revenue cycle management software built for medical billing teams — the people who turn claims into cash. Over the next ten minutes, I'll walk you through the complete workflow: from verifying a patient's eligibility, to submitting clean claims, to appealing every denial until it's paid. Everything you're about to see is the real product. Let's go.

## 02-login

We start at the login screen. Pulse is multi-tenant, so every billing company gets its own private workspace — your data never mixes with anyone else's. I'll sign in with our demo account. Notice there's no watered-down demo mode here — this is the exact login your team will use every morning.

## 03-briefing

This is the Daily Briefing — the first screen your billers should open every morning. It answers one question: what needs me today? Overdue follow-ups at the top, claims about to blow their timely filing deadline, unworked denials sorted by dollars at stake, and what the team recovered this week. One screen, zero hunting through reports.

## 04-worklist

My Work is the smart worklist — each biller's personal queue, scored by priority. Overdue follow-ups score highest, then filing deadlines at risk, then high-dollar denials, then stale A R. Your team simply works top to bottom. No more asking what to work on next — the system decides, by revenue impact.

## 05-claims

The Claims module is your system of record. Every claim shows its status, payer, billed amount, and filing countdown. See these badges? Amber means the filing deadline is within thirty days. Red means fourteen or fewer — and expired means that money is gone unless someone acts today. Select claims in bulk to mark them worked, scrub them, or export them.

## 06-scrubber

Before a claim ever reaches the payer, run the scrubber. Thirteen checks — procedure and diagnosis code validity, N P I format, billed amounts, filing deadlines, duplicates. Here I'm scrubbing a batch of claims. And there's a result: this claim is missing diagnosis codes. Fix it now, or the payer rejects it in two weeks and it comes back as a denial. Your choice.

## 07-edi837

Clean claims become 837 P files — the standard electronic format every clearinghouse accepts — with one click. Your billing N P I, taxonomy, and provider details are baked in from your organization settings. Download the file, upload it to your clearinghouse, done. No more retyping claims into a portal.

## 08-era835

When the payer responds, they send an 835 remittance file. Upload it here, and Pulse posts every payment automatically — paid in full, partial, or denied — extracting the payer, check number, and dates. It even reads the C A R C reason codes, so each denial lands with its real reason attached, and authorization-related denials are automatically linked to their authorization. A full day of manual posting, done in seconds.

## 09-appeals

Now the heart of the product: the denial appeal pipeline. Every denial becomes an appeal — assigned to a biller, prioritized, and tracked from open to won. This drawer is the appeal's home: the claim details, any related authorizations, a notes timeline of everything anyone has done, and canned note templates for the actions you repeat every day. Change the status, and Pulse schedules the follow-up automatically.

## 10-letters

And here's the part billers love. One click generates a payer-ready appeal letter. Pulse picks the right template for the denial reason — medical necessity, missing authorization, timely filing, and five more — then fills in your letterhead, the claim details, and the linked authorization number automatically. Copy it, print it, send it. And every template can be rewritten in your organization's own voice.

## 11-authz

Prior authorizations live here. Every request is tracked from draft to approved, with expiration countdowns so nothing lapses mid-treatment. Amber means fourteen days left, red means seven. And when a claim denies for missing authorization, Pulse surfaces the matching auth right inside the appeal — one click links them, and the appeal letter cites the authorization number by itself.

## 12-eligibility

The cheapest denial is the one that never happens. The eligibility log tracks coverage verification per patient — active, inactive, or not yet verified — with copay and deductible captured at the time of the check. Sort by not-verified before the visit, and you stop eligibility denials at the front desk instead of discovering them in A R ninety days later.

## 13-reports

The Reports Center gives managers five views: A R aging by payer, denial root-cause analysis, team productivity, collections, and filing compliance. Every report exports to C S V. This is how you answer where are we losing money in Monday's meeting — with data, not guesses.

## 14-playbook

The Payer Playbook is your team's shared brain. Aetna needs modifier 25 on this code. United's appeal fax number. Each payer's quirks, documented, searchable, and editable by the team. The tribal knowledge that used to walk out the door when someone quit now lives here — and new hires ramp up in days, not months.

## 15-templates

Under Settings, Letter Templates lets you rewrite every appeal letter — all eight denial categories, plus your signature block. Click any placeholder to drop in patient, claim, or organization details, and preview the letter with sample data before you save. Set it once, and every letter your team generates follows your voice. There's also a full audit log of every record viewed — your HIPAA trail, built in.

## 16-outro

So here's the full loop. Verify eligibility. Track the authorization. Scrub the claim. Export the 837. Post the 835. Appeal every denial with an auto-drafted letter. And watch it all in reports. Denied claims stop being write-offs and start being a workflow. That's Pulse R C M. Thanks for watching — and let's recover your revenue.

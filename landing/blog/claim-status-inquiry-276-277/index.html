276/277 Claim Status Inquiry Explained

ClaimPaid · October 24, 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

The 276 is the electronic claim status request; the 277 is the payer's response. Send a 276 with the claim's identifiers (member ID, dates of service, billed amount, claim number if you have one) and the 277 returns the claim's current status — received, pending, finalized — without a phone call. Use 276/277 batches for routine follow-up and reserve phone calls for stuck claims that need a human.

Claim follow-up is where billing teams lose their afternoons: one payer portal per payer, hold music per payer, a different rep with a different answer per call. The 276/277 transaction pair exists to replace most of that with a single electronic round-trip. If your clearinghouse supports it — most do — it's the highest-leverage EDI transaction most teams underuse.

What the 276 and 277 actually are

The pair is part of the same HIPAA X12 family as the 837, 835, 999, and 277CA:

  • 276 — Health Care Claim Status Request. You send it. It identifies the claim (member ID, dates of service, billed amount, claim control number if known) and asks: where is this claim?
  • 277 — Health Care Claim Status Notification. The payer sends it back. It reports the claim's status using standardized status category and status codes — received, pending/in process, finalized, and the adjudication outcome.

There's also the unsolicited 277 (277U), which some payers push proactively when a claim's status changes — but the workhorse is the solicited 276/277 request-response pair.

What's in a 277 response

The 277 returns the claim's status in standardized codes: a status category (the broad bucket — acknowledged/received, pending, finalized) and status codes with more specific detail. What you're really asking is one of three questions:

  • Did you get it? If the payer has no record of the claim, stop and check your 277CA acknowledgments and clearinghouse reports — the claim may never have arrived.
  • Is it moving? "Pending" with a recent status date is fine; "pending" unchanged for 45 days is a stuck claim.
  • Is it done? Finalized means there's an adjudication decision — go read the 835 instead of asking the rep to read it to you.

When the phone still wins

The 276/277 doesn't replace every call. Pick up the phone when the 277 status is stale or contradictory, when you need a supervisor-level decision (reprocessing requests, timely-filing disputes), or when the payer's 277 responses are unreliable — some payers' status data lags their internal systems by days. The right division of labor: 276/277 for routine status, phone for exceptions.

One 276 can replace a phone call — a batch of them replaces a whole afternoon of them.

Making 276/277 part of the workflow

  • Batch by age. Run 276 requests on a schedule: claims with no 277CA after 7 days, no 835 after 30 days, pending claims re-checked every 14 days.
  • Match the 277 to your worklist. Status responses should update claim work queues automatically — a "finalized" status with no matching 835 is itself a follow-up trigger.
  • Track payer responsiveness. If one payer's 277s are consistently stale, that's data for your payer performance tracking — and for deciding which claims genuinely need calls.
  • Confirm 276/277 support during enrollment. Not every payer supports the transaction for every line of business; verify before building the workflow around it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 276 transaction?

The 276 (Health Care Claim Status Request) is the HIPAA-standard electronic request asking a payer for a claim's current status. You send it with the claim's identifiers — member ID, dates of service, billed amount, and claim number if known.

What is a 277 transaction?

The 277 (Health Care Claim Status Notification) is the payer's response to a 276, reporting the claim's status with standardized status category and status codes — received, pending/in process, or finalized with the adjudication outcome.

Can 276/277 replace calling the payer?

For routine status checks, yes — that's what it's for. Keep phone calls for exceptions: stale or contradictory statuses, reprocessing requests, timely-filing disputes, and payers whose 277 data lags their internal systems.

How often should I run 276 status checks?

A common cadence: check claims with no acknowledgment after 7 days, no remittance after 30 days, and re-check pending claims every 14 days. Match 277 responses back to your work queues automatically.

Do all payers support 276/277?

No — support varies by payer and line of business. Confirm 276/277 support during payer enrollment before building follow-up workflows around it.

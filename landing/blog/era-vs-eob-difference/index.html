ERA vs EOB: What's the Difference?

ClaimPaid · October 23, 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

An ERA (Electronic Remittance Advice, the 835 EDI file) and an EOB (Explanation of Benefits, the paper or PDF document) communicate the same thing: how the payer adjudicated your claim — what was paid, what was denied, and why. The difference is format and usability. The ERA is structured data your practice management system can auto-post; the EOB is a human-readable document someone must read and key in manually.

After you submit a claim, the payer answers with a remittance: what was paid, what was denied, what was adjusted, and why. That answer arrives in one of two forms — the ERA or the EOB — and understanding both (and which one you're actually getting) is fundamental to posting payments, working denials, and reconciling revenue.

Definitions

  • ERA — Electronic Remittance Advice: the HIPAA-standard 835 EDI transaction. Structured, machine-readable data delivered electronically, usually through your clearinghouse.
  • EOB — Explanation of Benefits: the paper (or PDF) document — the familiar multi-page printout with patient, claim, and payment details in human-readable form. EOBs also go to patients, which is a common source of confusion; the provider version and the patient version describe the same adjudication.

Why ERAs matter more than EOBs operationally

Auto-posting is the headline benefit: when 835s flow into the practice management system and post automatically, payment posting labor drops dramatically and posting errors fall with it. But the deeper value is in denial workflow — structured CARC/RARC codes can route denials to the right work queues automatically, which is how teams track denial KPIs without drowning in paper.

EOBs still matter. They're the document of record patients see, they're what you pull when an ERA is ambiguous, and for some payers or claim types they're still all you get. But every payer you can get ERAs from is a payer you should get ERAs from.

Getting set up for ERA delivery

  1. Enroll per payer. ERA enrollment is typically separate from EFT (electronic funds transfer) enrollment and separate from claim submission enrollment. Many practices have EFT turned on but never completed ERA enrollment — they're getting fast money with slow information.
  2. Route through your clearinghouse. Your clearinghouse receives the 835s and delivers them to your PM system. Confirm the routing is active for each payer.
  3. Configure auto-posting rules in the PM system: which adjustments post automatically, which require review, and what happens with unmapped codes.
  4. Monitor unposted ERAs. An ERA that arrives but doesn't post is worse than a late EOB — it's invisible. Build a daily exception report.

Reading either one

Whether it's an ERA or an EOB, the anatomy is the same: claim identifiers, billed vs. allowed vs. paid amounts, adjustment reason codes (CARC) with remark codes (RARC), and patient responsibility. Learn to read the codes — our denial reasons guide covers the most common ones — and both documents become equally legible. The ERA just gets you there faster.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an ERA and an EOB?

An ERA (Electronic Remittance Advice, the 835 EDI file) is structured, machine-readable payment data delivered electronically. An EOB (Explanation of Benefits) is the human-readable paper or PDF version. Both communicate the same adjudication results — what was paid, denied, and why — but only the ERA can be auto-posted by software.

What is an 835 file?

The 835 is the HIPAA-standard EDI transaction for the Electronic Remittance Advice. Payers send it to communicate claim payment and denial decisions in structured format, typically delivered through a clearinghouse to the provider's practice management system.

Should I enroll in ERA, EFT, or both?

Both, and separately. EFT (electronic funds transfer) gets the money to your bank account fast; ERA enrollment gets the payment details to your system fast. Many practices enable EFT but never complete ERA enrollment — enroll in both, per payer.

Can ERAs be posted automatically?

Yes — that's their main operational advantage. With auto-posting rules configured in the practice management system, 835s can post payments and adjustments without manual keying. Always maintain a daily exception report for ERAs that arrive but fail to post.

Do patients get EOBs too?

Yes. Payers send EOBs to patients describing the same adjudication, which is a frequent source of confused patient phone calls. The provider ERA/EOB and the patient EOB describe the same claim decision.

What Is a Good Clean Claim Rate?

ClaimPaid · October 23, 2026 · 7 min read
The short answer

A clean claim rate measures the share of claims paid on first submission with no rework — calculated as clean claims divided by total claims submitted. Many practices treat 90% as a solid baseline and 95% or higher as excellent, though the right target depends on specialty and payer mix. The rate moves on three levers: front-end accuracy (eligibility, demographics, authorizations), coding accuracy, and pre-submission claim scrubbing.

Every billing team has a number that predicts its month: the clean claim rate. It's the percentage of claims that go out, get adjudicated, and get paid without anyone having to touch them again — no rejections, no denials, no rework. When this number is high, cash flows and staff spend their time on exceptions instead of re-doing routine work. When it's low, the whole revenue cycle drags.

How clean claim rate is calculated

The formula is simple:

Clean claim rate = (claims paid on first submission ÷ total claims submitted) × 100

A "clean" claim generally means one that passes payer edits on the first pass and pays without manual intervention. Definitions vary slightly — some organizations count claims accepted by the clearinghouse, others only count claims that actually paid — so the first step in benchmarking yourself is making sure you're measuring the same thing period to period. Pick one definition and stick with it.

Measure it monthly at minimum, and break it down by payer. A 93% overall rate can hide a payer sitting at 78% because of a specific edit or enrollment issue — and that payer is where your recovery opportunity lives.

What counts as a good clean claim rate?

There is no single official benchmark, but industry convention is well established: 90% is widely treated as a solid baseline, and 95% or higher is considered excellent. Practices below 85% usually have a systemic front-end problem — eligibility, registration data, or coding — rather than a payer problem.

Context matters. A specialty with complex prior authorization requirements or heavy Medicare secondary billing will run lower than a straightforward primary-care practice. Compare yourself against your own trend first, then against specialty peers if you have access to benchmark data. Direction matters more than any single month's number.

What drags the rate down

When clean claim rates slip, the causes cluster in a few familiar places:

  • Eligibility failures: coverage lapsed, wrong payer on file, coordination-of-benefits order wrong. Eligibility is the single biggest source of preventable rework — our eligibility verification checklist covers the front-desk discipline that fixes it.
  • Demographic errors: name mismatches, wrong date of birth, transposed member IDs. Small typos, big consequences.
  • Missing authorizations: services requiring prior auth submitted without one. See our prior authorization guide.
  • Coding errors: diagnosis codes that don't support medical necessity, missing modifiers, unbundled code pairs. Our modifiers guide covers the most common culprits.
  • Timely filing misses: claims submitted outside the payer's window are unclean by definition — and usually uncollectible. See timely filing limits by payer.

How to move the number

Improving clean claim rate is a front-end project, not a back-end one. The highest-ROI moves:

  1. Verify eligibility before every visit — real-time 270/271 checks, not batch the night before. Coverage changes constantly.
  2. Scrub every claim before submission. A claim scrubber runs payer-specific edits — missing data, coding conflicts, authorization checks — and flags problems while they're still cheap to fix.
  3. Track rejections by reason code. Clearinghouse rejections (as opposed to payer denials) are pure process failures. Categorize them weekly; the top three reasons usually explain most of the volume.
  4. Fix root causes, not just claims. If front-desk staff keep entering the wrong payer, that's a training or workflow problem. Correcting the claim without fixing the workflow guarantees next month looks the same.
  5. Watch it by payer. A payer-specific edit change can crater your rate overnight. Payer-level tracking turns a mystery into a ticket.

Clean claim rate vs. related metrics

Don't confuse clean claim rate with its cousins. First-pass resolution rate is similar but sometimes measured at the clearinghouse-acceptance stage rather than payment. Denial rate measures what came back denied — a high clean claim rate and a low denial rate usually travel together, but track both. The bottom line

Aim for 90% as your floor and 95% as your goal, measure it by payer every month, and treat every dip as a diagnostic signal. Clean claim rate is the closest thing revenue cycle has to a single health score — when it moves, everything downstream moves with it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good clean claim rate?

Many practices treat 90% as a solid baseline and 95% or higher as excellent, though the right target depends on specialty and payer mix. Below 85% usually indicates a systemic front-end problem with eligibility, registration data, or coding rather than a payer issue.

How is clean claim rate calculated?

Divide the number of claims paid on first submission (with no rework) by the total number of claims submitted, then multiply by 100. Define 'clean' consistently — some organizations count clearinghouse acceptance, others only count claims that actually paid.

What causes a low clean claim rate?

The most common causes are eligibility failures, demographic errors, missing prior authorizations, coding errors, and timely filing misses. Nearly all of these are front-end problems that pre-submission scrubbing and eligibility verification can prevent.

How can I improve my clean claim rate?

Verify eligibility before every visit with real-time checks, run every claim through a scrubber before submission, categorize clearinghouse rejections by reason code weekly, fix root-cause workflow problems rather than just correcting individual claims, and track the rate by payer to catch payer-specific issues fast.

Is clean claim rate the same as denial rate?

No. Clean claim rate measures claims paid on first submission without rework; denial rate measures the share of claims that came back denied. A high clean claim rate usually means a low denial rate, but they are different metrics and both are worth tracking.

What Is a Clearinghouse in Medical Billing?

ClaimPaid · October 24, 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

A clearinghouse sits between your practice and its payers: it translates claims into HIPAA-standard X12 EDI, runs front-end edits, routes each claim to the right payer, and returns acknowledgments (999 for file structure, 277CA for claim receipt). Most small practices submit through a clearinghouse rather than building direct connections to every payer — one integration instead of dozens.

Every electronic claim travels a path: your practice management system → somewhere → the payer. That "somewhere" is usually a clearinghouse. Understanding what it does — and what it doesn't do — explains half the mysteries in revenue cycle operations, from 999 rejections to missing 277CAs.

What a clearinghouse actually does

  • EDI translation. Converts your system's claim data into HIPAA-standard X12 transactions — the 837P/837I for claims — in the exact format each payer expects.
  • Front-end edits. Runs validity checks before the claim reaches the payer: required fields, valid codes, NPI formatting, payer-specific rules. Failures here come back as rejections — which is good, because they're same-day fixes.
  • Payer routing. Sends each claim to the right payer endpoint. One connection from you; the clearinghouse maintains the payer connections.
  • Acknowledgments. Returns the 999 (was the file structurally valid?) and the 277CA (did the payer accept the claim into its system?). These two files are your proof-of-submission paper trail.
  • ERA delivery. Most clearinghouses also route the 835 remittance advice back to you.

What a clearinghouse doesn't do

The clearinghouse is a courier with quality control, not a billing team. It doesn't decide medical necessity, doesn't appeal denials, doesn't verify eligibility for you (though many offer 270/271 eligibility as an add-on), and doesn't fix your coding. When a claim denies for CO-97 bundling, that's between you and the payer — the clearinghouse delivered it correctly.

Clearinghouse vs direct payer submission

  • Clearinghouse: one integration, standardized edits, consolidated acknowledgments, usually per-claim or subscription pricing. The right choice for most small and mid-size practices.
  • Direct: you build and maintain an EDI connection per payer, handle each payer's companion guide quirks yourself, and manage acknowledgments payer by payer. Large health systems and billing companies sometimes do this for their highest-volume payers to cut per-claim costs — but the maintenance burden is real.

Many organizations do both: clearinghouse for the long tail of payers, direct connections for the top few by volume.

One connection to a clearinghouse replaces dozens of direct payer connections.

Getting the most from your clearinghouse

  • Read your 277CAs. The 277CA is the payer's receipt — it's your timely-filing evidence. Archive it per claim.
  • Work rejections same-day. Clearinghouse rejections are pre-adjudication; the filing clock is running.
  • Don't confuse scrubbing layers. Your claim scrubber catches errors before the clearinghouse; the clearinghouse catches what you missed. Two layers beat one.
  • Enroll before you need it. Payer enrollment through a clearinghouse takes time — new payers and new providers should be enrolled before the first date of service, not after the first rejection.

Frequently asked questions

What is a clearinghouse in medical billing?

A clearinghouse is an intermediary that translates claims into HIPAA-standard X12 EDI, runs front-end validity edits, routes claims to the correct payers, and returns acknowledgments (999, 277CA) and remittance files (835).

Do I need a clearinghouse to submit claims?

Not strictly — direct payer submission is possible — but most small and mid-size practices use a clearinghouse because one integration replaces dozens of direct payer connections and their maintenance.

What is the difference between a 999 and a 277CA?

The 999 is a functional acknowledgment confirming the EDI file was structurally valid. The 277CA is a claim acknowledgment confirming the payer accepted the claim into its system. You need both: a valid file whose claims were all rejected still isn't filed.

Does a clearinghouse appeal denials?

No. A clearinghouse transmits and tracks claims; it doesn't dispute payer decisions. Denial appeals are your billing team's work (or your RCM software's).

How long does clearinghouse payer enrollment take?

It varies by payer — days to weeks. Enroll new payers and new providers before the first date of service, not after the first claim rejects.

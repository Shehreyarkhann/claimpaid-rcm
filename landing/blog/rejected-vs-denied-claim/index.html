Rejected vs Denied Claims: What's the Difference?

ClaimPaid · October 24, 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

A rejected claim never entered the payer's adjudication system — it failed front-end edits (bad NPI, invalid member ID, structural EDI errors). Fix the error and resubmit as a new claim; critically, the timely-filing clock keeps running while a claim sits rejected. A denied claim was adjudicated and refused with a reason code — fix or appeal per the code. Treating a rejection like a denial (appealing it) wastes weeks.

Teams use "rejected" and "denied" interchangeably, and it costs them. The two words describe completely different events with completely different next steps. Get the vocabulary right and half your "denial" queue turns out to be fix-and-resubmit work that never needed an appeal.

Rejected: never judged

A rejection happens before adjudication — at your clearinghouse's front-end edits or the payer's front-end system. The claim failed basic validity checks, so the payer never made a coverage or payment decision. Common causes:

  • Invalid or mismatched NPI, taxonomy, or provider identifiers
  • Member ID not found or inactive coverage on the date of service
  • Structural EDI errors — the file failed 999 validation
  • Missing required fields (diagnosis pointers, dates, place of service)

The fix: correct the data and resubmit as a new claim (frequency type 1, not 7 — nothing was adjudicated, so there's nothing to "correct"). Do not appeal a rejection — there's no decision to dispute.

2 different queues
Rejections go back to the front-end team for same-day resubmission. Denials go to the A/R team for coded follow-up. Mixing them up delays both.

Denied: judged and refused

A denial happens after adjudication — the payer processed the claim and refused payment (in whole or part) with a reason code: CO-16, CO-97, CO-29, PR-96, and the rest. Denials land on the 835 remittance advice with CAS adjustment segments. The fix depends on the code — see our denial reasons guide for the seven most common categories and their fixes.

The timely-filing trap

This is why the distinction matters financially: a rejected claim is not a filed claim. The payer has no record of it, so the timely-filing clock keeps running while it sits in your rejection queue. A claim rejected on day 85 of a 90-day window and ignored for a week becomes a timely-filing denial — unappealable — through pure inattention. Monitor rejections daily; they're the cheapest "denials" you'll ever prevent.

Rejected = never judged. Denied = judged and refused. Different problems, different fixes.

How to keep rejections near zero

  • Scrub before submission. A claim scrubber runs the same front-end edits the payer will — catching bad NPIs, invalid member IDs, and missing fields before transmission.
  • Verify eligibility at every visit. Most "member not found" rejections are stale coverage data; real-time 270/271 checks prevent them.
  • Work the rejection queue same-day. Rejections are same-day fixes, not A/R follow-up. A rejection older than 48 hours is a process failure.
  • Track rejection reasons. Recurring rejections are system problems (bad enrollment data, template errors) — fix them once upstream instead of fixing every claim downstream. That's what moves your clean claim rate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a rejected and denied claim?

A rejected claim never reached the payer's adjudication system — it failed front-end validity edits. A denied claim was adjudicated and refused with a reason code. Rejections are fixed and resubmitted; denials are fixed or appealed.

How do I fix a rejected claim?

Correct the underlying error (NPI, member ID, missing field, EDI structure) and resubmit as a new claim — frequency type 1, since nothing was adjudicated. Do not file it as a corrected claim or an appeal.

Can you appeal a rejected claim?

No — there's nothing to appeal. An appeal disputes a payer's adjudication decision, and a rejected claim was never adjudicated. Fix the data and resubmit.

Does a rejected claim count as filed for timely filing?

No. A rejected claim is not a filed claim — the payer has no record of it, and the timely-filing clock keeps running while it sits in the rejection queue. Work rejections same-day.

How do I reduce claim rejections?

Scrub claims before submission, verify eligibility with real-time 270/271 at every visit, work the rejection queue same-day, and fix recurring rejection causes upstream in your enrollment data and templates.

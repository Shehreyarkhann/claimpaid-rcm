ClaimPaid vs Legacy Billing Software

ClaimPaid · Updated October 28, 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

Don't compare billing software on feature checklists vendors write themselves. Compare on six criteria that predict whether you'll actually get value: can you trial it before committing, is pricing transparent, how does it help with coding accuracy, are BAAs built in, what can the AI write without your approval, and does an agent do the queue legwork — briefs, drafts, and predictions drawn from your own outcomes. Below is what to ask every vendor — including us — and our honest answers.

Most RCM software comparisons are secretly advertisements: a checklist where the author's product has every checkmark. This page is structured differently. Each criterion below has three columns — what to look for, where ClaimPaid fits (our real answers), and what to ask other vendors — so you can run the same evaluation on anyone. We don't name competitors or score them; the questions do the work.

Our honest framing: head-to-head "we're #1" claims are marketing, not information. Legacy billing software runs thousands of practices successfully — it tends to be comprehensive, proven, and expensive, with sales-led pricing and implementation timelines to match. What's changed is that newer tools compete on transparency, trialability, and AI agents that work the queue under human approval. Evaluate both generations against the same criteria below.

What to ask other vendors (copy-paste)

  1. Can I trial the full product for 30 days without a credit card or a sales call?
  2. What is the all-in monthly cost for a practice my size — including implementation, per-claim fees, and support tiers?
  3. When your AI coding assistant is uncertain, what exactly happens? Is the suggestion blocked, flagged, or filed?
  4. Is BAA execution part of the onboarding workflow, and can it be e-signed?
  5. What can your AI write or send without a human approving it first? Is every AI proposal, approval, and rejection recorded in an audit log?
  6. Does your AI learn from my data alone, or is it pooled with other customers? If you show predictions, do they come from my outcomes — and are they labeled with the evidence behind them?
  7. How do eligibility checks (270/271), claim status (276/277), and ERA (835) work — native, or via a third-party clearinghouse I contract separately?

Frequently asked questions

How should I compare RCM and medical billing software?

Compare on criteria that affect your daily work and total cost: whether you can trial it before buying, whether pricing is transparent, how it handles coding assistance and denials, whether BAAs are built in, and how the AI is governed — what it can write without a human approving it, whose data it learns from, and whether every action is audited.

Does ClaimPaid offer a free trial?

Yes. Selecting a plan starts a real 30-day trial of the app — no credit card required. Trial days remaining sync to billing, so checkout only starts paid billing after the trial ends.

How much does ClaimPaid cost?

RCM Starter is $199/month, RCM Pro + AI is $499/month, standalone ClaimPaid AI is $99/month, and Enterprise is custom pricing. All paid plans include a 1-month free trial.

Does ClaimPaid sign BAAs?

Yes — ClaimPaid includes built-in BAA e-signing, so the business associate agreement is executed inside the platform before any PHI flows.

Where does my data go when I use ClaimPaid's AI features?

The agent works inside your ClaimPaid workspace, using a frontier AI model via API to read your queue, draft, and answer. Three mechanics protect you: nothing the agent proposes is written without a human approving it, every agent action and decision is recorded in your audit log, and your workspace's data trains only your workspace's predictions — it is never pooled with other customers' data. We make no HIPAA certification claims: no such certification exists.

Does the AI send anything to payers?

No. The agent drafts and prepares — appeal letters, code sets, exception resolutions — and a person on your team approves each one and submits it through your current path. Nothing is transmitted to a payer by the AI.

When is ClaimPaid not the right fit?

ClaimPaid is software, not a billing service — if you want a company to do the billing work for you rather than software for your team, we're not that. It's web-based SaaS (not on-premise), and its payer content is built around US billing workflows.

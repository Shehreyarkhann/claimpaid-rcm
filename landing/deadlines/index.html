Timely filing deadlines by payer

Standard published limits · Updated October 29, 2026 · Your payer contract controls — verify before acting

Every payer gives you a fixed number of days from the date of service to get a claim filed. Miss it and the claim denies as CO-29 — time limit for filing expired — the one denial that is almost never overturned without proof the claim was submitted on time. These are the standard published limits ClaimPaid's deadline tracking uses; individual contracts, plan products, and state rules can shorten or lengthen them.

PayerStandard limitNotes
Aetna120 daysStandard commercial timely-filing limit, counted from the date of service. Some Aetna plans and state mandates differ.
Cigna180 daysStandard in-network commercial limit from the date of service for participating providers; non-participating timelines can be shorter.
UnitedHealthcare90 daysOne of the shortest standard windows among national payers — 90 days from the date of service for most commercial plans, which is why UHC claims dominate deadline risk in most A/R.
Anthem180 daysStandard limit from the date of service across most Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield plans; individual state plans vary.
Medicare365 daysFederal rule: claims must be filed no later than 12 months — or the end of the calendar year following the year of service, whichever is later — from the date of service. The audit tool uses 365 days as the working figure.
NY Medicaid90 daysNew York Medicaid requires initial submission within 90 days of the date of service; claims denied or rejected must be resubmitted within strict secondary windows.
Humana180 daysStandard commercial and Medicare Advantage limit from the date of service for most contracts.
Other payer365 daysWorking default for payers not listed. Many regional plans, Medicaid MCOs, and workers' compensation carriers use 90–180 days — find the number in your contract before relying on the default.

How the deadline is computed

Deadline = date of service + the payer's limit in days. The claim has to be received and accepted by the payer by that date. Three consequences follow:

  • Rejected is not filed. A claim rejected by the clearinghouse or the payer's front-end edits never starts the clock-stopping process — it is an unfiled claim with the deadline still approaching.
  • Wrong payer, same clock. Billing the wrong plan first (see CO-109) does not pause the correct payer's window.
  • Appeals have their own windows. Appeal and corrected-claim deadlines are usually counted from the remittance date and set by your contract — they are separate from, and often shorter than, the initial filing limit.
The disclaimer that matters: Deadlines shown are the standard published timely-filing limits. Your contract with each payer controls the actual deadline for your claims — verify before acting on any specific claim.

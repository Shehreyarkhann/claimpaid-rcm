What Is Medicare's Timely Filing Limit?

Standard published limits · Updated November 6, 2026 · Your payer contract controls — verify before acting

Medicare's timely filing limit is set by federal regulation (42 CFR 424.44): claims must be filed no later than December 31 of the calendar year following the year of service — and for services furnished October 1 through December 31, no later than December 31 of the second following year. In practice, treat the limit as 12 months (365 days) from the date of service and file well inside it.

How Medicare counts the clock

  • The October–December extension is real but narrow. It applies to the filing deadline only — it does not extend appeal or reopening clocks.
  • Reopening and appeal are different tracks. A billing error on a timely claim is a reopening; a disagreement with the determination is a redetermination. Filing one does not stop the other's clock.
  • "Received" is presumed 5 days after the notice date for the redetermination clock, unless you can show it arrived later.

If you miss Medicare's deadline

Medicare almost never excuses a late claim: outside narrow exceptions (such as an error by Medicare or its contractor), a claim filed after the calendar-year deadline is denied and cannot be billed to the beneficiary. The protection is procedural — file early, and keep the acceptance acknowledgment for every submission.

The disclaimer that matters: Deadlines shown are the standard published timely-filing limits. Your contract with the payer controls the actual deadline for your claims — verify before acting on any specific claim.

Questions

What is Medicare's timely filing limit?

Claims must be filed no later than December 31 of the calendar year following the year of service; services furnished October 1–December 31 have until December 31 of the second following year (42 CFR 424.44). Treat it as 12 months from the date of service in practice.

What is Medicare's corrected claim deadline?

Correcting a timely-filed claim is a reopening: within 1 year of the initial determination for any reason, or within 4 years for good cause (42 CFR 405.980). A reopening cannot add a never-billed service after the filing limit.

How long do you have to appeal a Medicare denial?

120 calendar days from receipt of the initial determination notice to request a redetermination, the first of five appeal levels (42 CFR 405.942).

What happens if you miss Medicare's filing deadline?

The claim is denied as untimely and, outside narrow exceptions such as Medicare contractor error, cannot be recovered — and the beneficiary cannot be billed for it.

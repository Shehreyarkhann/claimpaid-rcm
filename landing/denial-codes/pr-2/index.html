PR-2: Coinsurance amount (patient responsibility)

Group PR — Patient Responsibility · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What PR-2 means

Coinsurance amount. After any deductible, the plan splits the allowed amount by percentage — this is the patient's percentage share.

What it usually means on a real remittance

Like PR-1, PR-2 is normal cost-sharing on a paid claim, not a denial. It becomes a problem only when the percentage, tier, or out-of-pocket status behind it is wrong — or when the balance never gets worked as patient A/R.

Common causes

  • Standard plan cost-sharing on a correctly adjudicated claim — the expected case
  • The wrong coinsurance percentage was applied (in-network vs. out-of-network tier, or a service-specific rate)
  • The patient's out-of-pocket maximum was already reached, so coinsurance should have been $0
  • The claim priced at the wrong allowed amount, which scales the coinsurance with it

How to fix or appeal PR-2

  1. Verify the math: coinsurance percentage from the plan × the allowed amount, and check the out-of-pocket accumulator
  2. If correct, move the balance to patient responsibility and statement it while the service is recent
  3. If the tier or percentage is wrong, appeal with the plan's cost-share schedule and the eligibility evidence
  4. If the out-of-pocket max was met, dispute with the accumulator record — coinsurance after the max is a payer error
  5. Track patient-responsibility aging separately from payer A/R; PR balances need statements and payment plans, not appeals workflows
Corrected claim or appeal? Bill the patient at the plan's percentage. Appeal only a wrong tier, a wrong percentage, or coinsurance charged after the out-of-pocket maximum.

PR-2 — frequently asked questions

How is PR-2 different from PR-1 and PR-3?

Deductible (PR-1) is a fixed amount the patient pays first each year before the plan pays. Coinsurance (PR-2) is a percentage split of the allowed amount after the deductible. Copayment (PR-3) is a flat per-visit amount. A single claim can carry all three.

When is a PR-2 wrong?

When the percentage doesn't match the plan's cost-share for that service and network tier, when the out-of-pocket maximum was already met, or when the underlying allowed amount is wrong (fixing the allowance fixes the coinsurance).

How do I know the right coinsurance before billing?

The eligibility response: it returns the coinsurance percentage and out-of-pocket status for the plan, so the patient's share can be estimated and often collected at the point of service.

CO-197: Prior authorization or notification was absent

Group CO — Contractual Obligation · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What CO-197 means

Precertification, prior authorization, or notification was absent for a service that required it. The payer is denying the claim because approval was never obtained — or never matched to the claim — before the service was provided.

What it usually means on a real remittance

CO-197 typically denies the entire claim, not a single line, and it clusters on imaging, surgeries, injections, sleep studies, and specialty drugs. Because the failure happened before the service, this is one of the most preventable high-dollar denials in any A/R.

Common causes

  • The service required prior authorization and none was requested before the date of service
  • An authorization was obtained but the claim was billed under a different procedure code, units, or date range than approved
  • The authorization is on file with the payer but the auth number was missing from the claim
  • The rendering provider or facility on the claim differs from the provider named on the authorization

How to fix or appeal CO-197

  1. Check your own records first: was there an authorization? If yes, confirm the number, approved codes, units, and date span against what was billed
  2. If an auth exists but wasn't on the claim, submit a corrected claim with the authorization number in the correct field
  3. If no auth exists, check the payer's retro-authorization rules and the clinical circumstances — urgent/emergent care often has a retro path with documentation of the emergency
  4. If retro-auth is refused, appeal with the clinical record showing why the service couldn't wait, and any eligibility evidence you relied on
  5. Fix the system: verify authorization requirements at scheduling for every service on the payer's auth list, and hold claims at the scrubber when a required auth isn't attached
Corrected claim or appeal? Corrected claim if a valid auth exists but wasn't referenced. Appeal with a retro-authorization request if none exists — success depends on the payer's retro policy and whether the care was urgent.

CO-197 — frequently asked questions

Can I get a retroactive authorization after a CO-197?

Sometimes. Many payers allow retro-authorization within a set window when the service was urgent or emergent, or when eligibility couldn't be verified in advance. Elective services with no auth and no emergency are rarely forgiven. Ask for the payer's retro-auth policy in writing before spending appeal time.

What's the difference between CO-197 and CO-252?

CO-197 means no authorization existed or none could be matched. CO-252 means the payer wants documentation attached before it will adjudicate. One is a missing approval; the other is a missing attachment.

How do practices prevent CO-197?

An authorization tracker that ties every scheduled service to an approved auth — with the code, units, and date range visible before the claim is built — and a scrubber rule that holds any claim whose code requires auth when no auth is linked.

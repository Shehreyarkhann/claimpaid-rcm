PR-1: Deductible amount (patient responsibility)

Group PR — Patient Responsibility · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What PR-1 means

Deductible amount. The payer applied this portion of the allowed amount to the patient's annual deductible. The claim was adjudicated correctly under the plan design — this share is the patient's to pay, not the payer's.

What it usually means on a real remittance

PR-1 appears on paid claims, most heavily in the first quarter when deductibles reset. It isn't a denial: it's cost-sharing. The revenue risk is operational — deductible balances that sit unworked while the year (and the patient's memory of the service) moves on.

Common causes

  • The plan has an unmet deductible for the year — the most common and legitimate cause
  • The claim was applied to the deductible in error (service exempt from deductible, or deductible already met through another claim)
  • Preventive services that should be deductible-exempt were processed as deductible-applicable
  • The deductible was met but the payer's accumulator is behind and hasn't credited earlier claims

How to fix or appeal PR-1

  1. Check the patient's deductible accumulator: how much has been credited this year, and does this claim's application match the plan's rules?
  2. If the accumulator is right, transfer the balance to patient responsibility promptly and statement it — deductible balances age like any other A/R
  3. If the deductible was already met or the service is exempt, appeal with the evidence of the earlier satisfying claims or the plan's exemption rule
  4. Collect deductible information at scheduling for early-year services so patients hear it from you before the remittance, not after
  5. Don't write PR amounts off as contractual adjustments — misclassified PR-1s hide real collectible revenue in your adjustment reports
Corrected claim or appeal? Bill the patient. Appeal only an accumulator error — deductible already met or an exempt service misapplied.

The deadline angle

Every remedy above runs against a clock. Corrected claims and appeals have to land inside the payer's filing and appeal windows, and those windows are counted from the date of service — see the standard timely filing limits by payer and verify against your contract. PR-1 — frequently asked questions

What does PR-1 mean for who pays?

PR (Patient Responsibility) instead of CO (Contractual Obligation): the amount isn't written off — it's owed by the patient under the plan's cost-sharing design, and the claim 'paid' exactly as the plan intends. Billing the patient is the correct next step, not an appeal.

When should I dispute a PR-1?

When the payer's deductible accumulator is wrong: the deductible was already satisfied by earlier claims, or the service is deductible-exempt under the plan (many preventive services are). Appeal with the accumulator evidence.

How do I handle deductibles better?

Accurate deductible status at eligibility — the eligibility response reports remaining deductible. Verifying before service lets you collect at the point of care instead of billing into January's accumulator confusion weeks later.

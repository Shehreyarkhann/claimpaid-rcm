CO-22: Another payer is primary (coordination of benefits)

Group CO — Contractual Obligation · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What CO-22 means

This care may be covered by another payer per coordination of benefits. The payer you billed believes it is secondary — another insurer is primary for this patient and must adjudicate first.

What it usually means on a real remittance

CO-22 is a sequencing denial, not a value denial: the money is usually recoverable, but only after the claim goes to the right payer in the right order. It shows up heavily in Medicare Secondary Payer situations, working spouses, and accident-related care.

Common causes

  • The patient has a primary plan (employer coverage, auto, workers' comp, Medicare secondary situations) that must adjudicate first
  • Medicare is secondary but the claim went to Medicare first — common with working-aged, disability, and end-stage renal disease coordination rules
  • The primary payer denied or paid, but the primary EOB wasn't attached to the secondary claim
  • The patient's coverage changed and the eligibility check at intake captured the wrong primary

How to fix or appeal CO-22

  1. Identify the correct primary payer from the patient's coverage: employer plan, spouse's plan, auto/liability for accident-related care, or Medicare Secondary Payer rules
  2. Bill the primary payer first (or confirm its adjudication if it already processed)
  3. Attach or transmit the primary payer's payment and adjustment information with the secondary claim — secondary cannot price the claim without it
  4. If primary information was wrong, update the patient's coverage record so every open claim gets re-sequenced, not just this one
  5. Appeal only when the payer's COB determination is factually wrong (e.g. the 'primary' policy was terminated) — with termination evidence attached
Corrected claim or appeal? Neither an appeal nor a corrected claim to the same payer — bill the primary payer, then resubmit to this payer with the primary remittance attached.

The deadline angle

Every remedy above runs against a clock. Corrected claims and appeals have to land inside the payer's filing and appeal windows, and those windows are counted from the date of service — see the standard timely filing limits by payer and verify against your contract. CO-22 — frequently asked questions

What does the secondary payer need from me?

The primary payer's remittance (835/EOB) must accompany the secondary claim, showing what primary paid and the remaining patient or secondary responsibility. Submitting to secondary without it is the most common reason CO-22 repeats.

When is Medicare secondary?

Medicare is primary in most cases, but secondary when the patient is covered by an employer group plan through current employment (themselves or a spouse), in many disability and ESRD coordination periods, and when liability/no-fault coverage applies. The Medicare Secondary Payer rules decide — checking at intake prevents the denial.

How do I prevent CO-22?

Eligibility verification that returns coordination-of-benefits information — other coverage on file — before the first claim goes out, plus an intake question about accidents and other insurance for every new patient.

CO-29: The time limit for filing has expired

Group CO — Contractual Obligation · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What CO-29 means

The time limit for filing has expired. The payer received the claim after its timely filing deadline — counted from the date of service under your contract — and denies it in full.

What it usually means on a real remittance

CO-29 is the most expensive denial in this library because it is nearly absolute: the service was real, the coverage was real, and the money is gone anyway. It is also the denial most often discovered months too late, sitting at the bottom of an aging report nobody sorted by deadline.

Common causes

  • The claim sat in a work queue past the payer's filing limit — the classic silent expiry in unmanaged A/R
  • A rejected claim was never reworked: clearinghouse rejections don't count as filed, and the resubmission came after the deadline
  • The claim was billed to the wrong payer first; by the time it reached the right payer, its window had closed
  • Corrected claims and rebills sent after the original window closed, without proof the original was timely

How to fix or appeal CO-29

  1. Before anything else, search for proof of timely submission: clearinghouse acceptance reports, 277CA files, payer portal submission records, certified mail receipts
  2. If proof exists, appeal immediately with the proof attached and the deadline math shown: date of service, payer limit, date received
  3. If the claim was first billed to the wrong payer, attach the original timely submission to that payer — some contracts honor the first-billing date; many don't, but it's the only argument available
  4. If there is no proof and the claim was late, write it off per policy and record the cause — the value of a CO-29 is the process failure it exposes
  5. Prevent the next one: work A/R by days-to-deadline rather than by balance or alphabet, and treat clearinghouse rejections as unfiled claims with the clock still running
Corrected claim or appeal? Appeal ONLY with documentary proof of timely submission. Without proof, no appeal — write it off and fix the queue discipline that let it expire.

The deadline angle

Every remedy above runs against a clock. Corrected claims and appeals have to land inside the payer's filing and appeal windows, and those windows are counted from the date of service — see the standard timely filing limits by payer and verify against your contract. CO-29 — frequently asked questions

Can a timely filing denial be overturned?

Rarely, and only with proof: clearinghouse acceptance reports, 277CA acknowledgments, or certified-mail receipts showing the payer received the claim inside the window. 'We sent it' without a receipt loses. Genuinely late claims are almost never overturned.

What's the real fix for CO-29?

The claim itself — a denial you can prevent only by filing on time. That's why deadline tracking matters more than appeal skill here: the fix happens before the denial exists. Where do I find my payer's actual filing limit?

Each payer sets its own limit in your provider contract — standard published windows (UHC 90 days, Aetna 120, Medicare 365) are the starting point, and the contract controls. Our deadlines hub lists the standard limits and how they're counted.

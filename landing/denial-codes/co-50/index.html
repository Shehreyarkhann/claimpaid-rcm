CO-50: Not deemed medically necessary by the payer

Group CO — Contractual Obligation · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What CO-50 means

This is a non-covered service because it is not deemed a medical necessity by the payer. The payer is not saying the service isn't in the benefit plan — it's saying the documentation doesn't establish that this patient needed it.

What it usually means on a real remittance

CO-50 appears most often on imaging, injections, therapy, durable medical equipment, and higher-level E/M services. The whole line denies, usually with no patient responsibility for participating providers — the loss lands on the practice unless the appeal succeeds.

Common causes

  • The diagnosis on the claim does not support the service under the payer's medical policy or LCD/NCD
  • Documentation is thin: the note doesn't show failed conservative treatment, severity, or why this service was required
  • The service is on the payer's list of treatments requiring specific criteria (advanced imaging, injections, certain therapies) that weren't demonstrated
  • Frequency or setting doesn't match policy — the policy covers the service, just not as often or not in that setting

How to fix or appeal CO-50

  1. Get the payer's actual medical policy or the Medicare LCD/NCD for the code and read the coverage criteria line by line against your documentation
  2. Identify exactly which criterion the record fails to demonstrate — that gap is the whole appeal
  3. Build the appeal around the missing evidence: notes, prior treatment history, test results, and the treating provider's statement of medical necessity
  4. If the documentation genuinely doesn't support the service, document the write-off decision and fix the front end: policy-based necessity checks at scheduling and order entry
  5. For recurring CO-50s on the same code, create a documentation template that forces the policy criteria into the note before the service is rendered
Corrected claim or appeal? Appeal with documentation — this is a judgment denial, not a clerical one. A corrected claim only helps if the wrong diagnosis was attached; otherwise the clinical record is the argument.

CO-50 — frequently asked questions

How is CO-50 different from CO-96 (non-covered)?

CO-50 means the payer reviewed the service against its medical policy and decided the record doesn't support it. CO-96 is a benefit denial — the service isn't covered under the plan at all, regardless of necessity. The distinction decides whether documentation can fix it (CO-50, sometimes) or not (CO-96).

What documentation wins a CO-50 appeal?

The clinical record: progress notes showing the condition and its severity, prior conservative treatment and its outcome, relevant imaging or lab results, and a clear statement of why this service was the appropriate next step. Appeals that restate the claim without adding clinical evidence are denied again.

When is a CO-50 not worth appealing?

When the service falls outside the plan's benefits or the payer's policy excludes it categorically. Then the path is checking patient responsibility rules and your contract — not repeated appeals.

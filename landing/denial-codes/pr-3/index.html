PR-3: Copayment amount (patient responsibility)

Group PR — Patient Responsibility · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What PR-3 means

Copayment amount. The flat per-visit or per-service amount the plan assigns to the patient — the copay shown on the member's card and benefit summary.

What it usually means on a real remittance

PR-3 is routine cost-sharing. Its classic failure mode is internal, not payer-side: the copay was collected at check-in and then the remittance's PR-3 gets posted as a fresh balance, double-billing the patient.

Common causes

  • The standard copay for the visit type under the patient's plan — the normal case
  • The copay was applied to a service that should be copay-exempt (many preventive services)
  • A copay was collected at the front desk AND applied again on the remittance, double-counting the patient's share
  • The wrong copay tier was applied (specialist vs. primary care, or facility vs. office)

How to fix or appeal PR-3

  1. Confirm the copay amount matches the plan's copay for that visit type and setting
  2. Reconcile with front-desk collections: if the patient paid at check-in, the payment should satisfy this PR-3 — make sure posting doesn't create a second balance
  3. If the service was copay-exempt or the wrong tier applied, appeal with the plan's benefit summary
  4. Transfer any genuinely owed remainder to patient responsibility and statement it
  5. Audit copay posting monthly: double-applied copays are a quiet source of patient complaints and refunds
Corrected claim or appeal? Reconcile and bill the patient for any true remainder. Appeal only a wrong tier or a copay applied to an exempt service.

The deadline angle

Every remedy above runs against a clock. Corrected claims and appeals have to land inside the payer's filing and appeal windows, and those windows are counted from the date of service — see the standard timely filing limits by payer and verify against your contract. PR-3 — frequently asked questions

The patient already paid the copay at the desk — why is PR-3 on the remittance?

Reconcile: the remittance applies the copay once. If the patient already paid it at check-in, the account should show the payment satisfying the PR-3 amount — if your posting treats both as separate balances, the patient gets billed twice for the same copay. That's a posting fix, not a payer dispute.

When should I dispute PR-3?

When the tier or the exemption is wrong: specialist copay applied to a primary care visit, or a copay applied to a copay-exempt preventive service. The plan's benefit summary is the evidence.

How do I collect the right copay up front?

The eligibility/benefit response states the copay by service type. Verifying before the visit means collecting the right amount once, at the desk, instead of reconciling later.

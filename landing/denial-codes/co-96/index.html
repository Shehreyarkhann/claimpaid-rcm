CO-96: Non-covered charge(s)

Group CO — Contractual Obligation · Denial Code Library · Updated October 29, 2026

What CO-96 means

Non-covered charge(s). The service billed is not a benefit under the patient's plan — the plan doesn't pay for this service for this member, regardless of medical necessity or documentation.

What it usually means on a real remittance

CO-96 denies the line in full. Unlike CO-50 (medical necessity), no amount of clinical documentation creates a benefit that doesn't exist. The questions are whether the exclusion is real, whether the right plan was billed, and who bears the cost under your contract.

Common causes

  • The service is excluded from the patient's plan (a category the plan simply doesn't cover)
  • The benefit exists but has conditions that weren't met — site of service, network tier, or plan-specific requirements
  • The claim was billed to a plan product the patient isn't enrolled in, where the service would be covered under their actual product
  • The service is statutorily excluded for the payer type (e.g. certain services under Medicare)

How to fix or appeal CO-96

  1. Confirm the exclusion is real: check the plan's benefit summary and your contract for the service, setting, and patient plan product
  2. Verify the claim went to the right product — a service excluded under one plan design may be covered under the patient's actual plan
  3. If the service is truly non-covered, follow your payer contract and financial policy on patient responsibility, including any required advance waiver
  4. If the payer misapplied its own benefit design, appeal with the plan document pages that show the benefit
  5. Add benefit verification for your top non-covered services to the scheduling workflow so the conversation happens before the service, not after the denial
Corrected claim or appeal? Usually neither — verify the benefit, then follow the patient-responsibility path your contract allows. Appeal only when the payer misapplied its own plan design.

The deadline angle

Corrected claims and appeals have to land inside the payer's filing and appeal windows, and those windows are counted from the date of service — see the standard timely filing limits by payer and verify against your contract. CO-96 — frequently asked questions

How is CO-96 different from CO-50?

CO-96 says the service isn't a covered benefit under the plan — medical evidence doesn't change that. CO-50 says the service is a benefit but wasn't medically necessary for this patient — evidence is the whole game. Misreading one for the other wastes appeal cycles.

Can I bill the patient after a CO-96?

Check the contract and plan rules first: if the service is genuinely excluded, patient billing usually follows your financial policy and any advance-notice (ABN-style or waiver) rules for the payer type. For participating providers, some exclusions still can't be billed to the patient — the contract decides.

How do I prevent CO-96?

Verify benefits for the specific service — not just 'active coverage' — before rendering non-urgent care, and give the patient a cost estimate and waiver where the payer type requires one. Eligibility checks that return benefit-level detail catch many CO-96s in advance.
